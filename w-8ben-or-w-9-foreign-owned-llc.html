W-8BEN or W-9: Which Form Does a Foreign-Owned Single-Member LLC Give Clients?

📝🇺🇸🌍

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: If your US single-member LLC is a disregarded entity and you, the owner, are not a US person, you should not give clients or platforms a Form W-9. The IRS Form W-9 instructions say the foreign owner must complete an appropriate Form W-8 instead, even though the LLC was formed in the US and even if the owner has a US tax number. For an individual owner that is usually Form W-8BEN.

Why the LLC itself is not the one that signs

A single-member LLC that has not elected to be taxed as a corporation is "disregarded" for US income tax purposes. The IRS treats its income as the owner's income. That is why tax forms ask about the owner, not the LLC:

  • The W-9 instructions say the owner's name goes on line 1 and the disregarded entity's name goes on line 2.
  • They add that if the owner of the disregarded entity is a foreign person, the owner must complete a Form W-8 instead of a W-9, "even if the foreign person has a U.S. TIN."
  • The W-8BEN instructions say the single owner of a disregarded entity is treated as the beneficial owner of the income and, rather than the disregarded entity itself, submits the form.

Which W-8 form to use

  • Form W-8BEN: for a nonresident alien individual. This is the usual case when one person living abroad owns the LLC.
  • Form W-8BEN-E: for a foreign entity, such as a foreign company that owns the LLC. A disregarded entity generally does not use W-8BEN-E for itself, except in limited cases such as documenting its own FATCA status.
  • Form W-8ECI: if the income is effectively connected with a US trade or business. The IRS instructions say W-8BEN is not valid for that income, and W-8ECI should be used instead.

Whether your income is effectively connected with a US trade or business depends on your facts, such as where you and any staff do the work. If you are unsure, ask a US tax professional before choosing between W-8BEN and W-8ECI.

Who gets the form

You give the form to the client, marketplace or payment platform that asks for it. Do not send it to the IRS. They keep it to decide whether to withhold tax and how to report payments to you.

How long a W-8BEN stays valid

A W-8BEN generally remains valid until the last day of the third calendar year after the year you sign it. A form signed in 2026, for example, is generally valid through December 31, 2029. If your circumstances change, such as moving country, you give a new form.

When a W-9 is the right form

Form W-9 is only for US persons: US citizens, resident aliens and entities treated as US persons. A US LLC owned by a non-resident becomes a W-9 filer only if its tax status changes, for example if it elects to be taxed as a US corporation. A multi-member LLC taxed as a partnership is also a US person, but it checks box 3b on the W-9 when it has foreign partners and is providing the form to another partnership, trust or estate it holds an interest in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreign-owned US LLC fill out Form W-9?

Not if it is a single-member disregarded entity. The IRS W-9 instructions say a disregarded US entity with a foreign owner must use the appropriate Form W-8.

Whose name goes on Form W-8BEN for my LLC?

The owner's. The W-8BEN instructions say the single owner of a disregarded entity is the beneficial owner of the income and submits the form.

Does having an EIN or ITIN mean I should use a W-9?

No. The W-9 instructions say a foreign owner uses a Form W-8 even if they have a US taxpayer identification number.

When should I use Form W-8ECI instead of W-8BEN?

When the income is effectively connected with a US trade or business. Whether yours is depends on your facts, so check with a tax professional.

Do I send Form W-8BEN to the IRS?

No. You give it to the client or platform that requests it.
