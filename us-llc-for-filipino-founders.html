How Filipino Founders Can Form and Run a US LLC (2026 Guide)

🇵🇭🇺🇸🧾

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: Yes. A Filipino citizen living in the Philippines can form and own a US LLC, with no US citizenship or residency needed. At home, resident citizens are taxed on income from sources within and outside the Philippines, so LLC profits belong in your Philippine tax picture. The US and the Philippines have an income tax treaty. On the US side, you need an EIN, a yearly Form 5472 and a US business bank account.

Why Filipino founders form US LLCs

The Philippines has a large remote work, virtual assistant, BPO and e-commerce community serving US clients. A US LLC lets those businesses contract as a US entity and use US payment processors. The Philippines is not covered by any of the US Treasury's country-based sanctions programs.

Philippine tax: worldwide income for resident citizens

Under Section 23 of the National Internal Revenue Code, as described in BIR issuances, a citizen of the Philippines residing in the Philippines is taxable on all income from sources within and without the Philippines. Non-resident citizens and aliens are taxed on Philippine-source income only. For founders this means:

  • Profits from your US LLC are generally part of your Philippine taxable income if you are a resident citizen.
  • Foreign taxes you pay may be creditable, within limits, if you can document the foreign income and the tax paid.
  • Resident citizens with business income inside and outside the Philippines are among the filers the BIR directs to the business income tax return.

How the LLC itself is treated for Philippine purposes, and which return and rates apply, is a question for a Philippine tax adviser or CPA.

Sending capital from the Philippines

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) regulates foreign exchange, and banks follow its rules when residents buy foreign currency to invest abroad. Expect your bank to ask about the purpose of the transfer and for supporting documents. Ask your bank what it needs before you capitalize the LLC.

The US-Philippines tax treaty

The IRS lists an income tax treaty between the United States and the Philippines. It can matter for certain US-source income, but whether it helps depends on your facts. Treaty claims usually need a US taxpayer identification number such as an ITIN.

US filing duties

  • EIN: apply with Form SS-4 without an SSN. See how to get an EIN without an SSN.
  • Form 5472 and pro forma 1120: due each year for a foreign-owned single-member LLC, even with no income. The IRS penalty for not filing is $25,000. See Form 5472 for foreign-owned LLCs.
  • BOI: US-created LLCs are exempt under FinCEN's final rule, effective August 14, 2026. See BOI reporting in 2026.
  • ITIN: if you must file a personal US return or claim treaty benefits. See ITIN for non-residents.

Banking, Stripe and addresses

Stripe does not list the Philippines as a supported country. Filipino founders often apply for a US Stripe account through their US LLC, with a US bank account in the LLC's name. Stripe reviews the business and owners and can ask for more information. See Stripe for non-US LLC owners.

US banks and fintechs publish their own eligibility rules for non-resident owners, and those rules change. Check the provider's page before applying, and read opening a US business bank account as a non-resident. Your personal address should be your actual home in the Philippines. For the LLC's business address, banks frequently decline CMRA and virtual mailbox addresses. See what a CMRA address is.

Which state

Many Filipino founders choose Wyoming for its $100 formation fee and $60 minimum annual report tax. Delaware is the usual choice for startups planning to raise US funding. See Wyoming vs Delaware.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Filipino living in the Philippines own a US LLC?

Yes. US states do not require LLC owners to be US citizens or residents.

Do I pay Philippine tax on my US LLC income?

Resident citizens are taxed on income from all sources, inside and outside the Philippines. Confirm how your LLC income is reported with a Philippine CPA.

Does the US have a tax treaty with the Philippines?

Yes. The IRS lists a US income tax treaty with the Philippines.

Is Stripe available in the Philippines?

Stripe does not list the Philippines as a supported country. A US LLC with a US bank account can apply for a US Stripe account, subject to Stripe's review.
