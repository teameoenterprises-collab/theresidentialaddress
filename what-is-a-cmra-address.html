What Is a CMRA Address and Why Do Banks Flag It?

📮🔍🏦

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: A CMRA (Commercial Mail Receiving Agency) is a business registered with the USPS to receive mail for other people, such as a virtual mailbox, a UPS Store or a mailbox rental shop. The USPS marks these addresses in its database, so banks, fintechs and payment processors can see instantly that the address is a mail drop, not a place where the business operates. Many of them decline CMRA addresses as a company's address.

How CMRAs work

Anyone renting a box at a CMRA files USPS Form 1583, authorising the CMRA to accept mail for them. The CMRA's street address then serves many unrelated customers, each with a "suite" or "PMB" number.

How banks detect a CMRA address

Address verification tools check the USPS database, which flags each address as commercial or residential and whether it is a CMRA. This happens automatically during onboarding, before anyone at the bank reviews the application.

Which addresses are usually CMRAs

  • Virtual mailbox services that scan and forward mail.
  • UPS Store and similar mailbox rental shops.
  • Many "virtual office" packages.

Registered agent addresses are not always CMRAs, but they are shared by thousands of companies, so banks treat them the same way for the company's address.

What to use instead

For the company's address, banks want a real place connected to the business: an office, a coworking membership with its own lease, or a residential property with a lease. Whatever you use, keep the documents that tie the LLC to it. For your personal address on the application, use your actual home address, which can be outside the US.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does CMRA stand for?

Commercial Mail Receiving Agency: a business registered with the USPS to receive mail on behalf of others, such as a virtual mailbox or UPS Store.

How can I check if an address is a CMRA?

USPS address verification tools and many address-validation services report whether an address is flagged as a CMRA. Banks run this check automatically.

Can I use a virtual mailbox for my LLC?

You can use one to receive mail, but many banks and payment processors decline it as the company's address because it is flagged as a CMRA.
