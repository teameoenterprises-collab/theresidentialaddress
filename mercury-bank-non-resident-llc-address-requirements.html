Mercury Bank Address Requirements for a Non-Resident LLC

🏦📍🌍

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: Mercury asks a non-resident LLC for two different addresses. The company needs a principal place of business, which can be in the US or abroad and can be residential, but cannot be a registered agent address, a PO box or a UPS Store address. Each owner of 25% or more must give the physical address where they actually live, and mail drops, commercial mail receiving agencies and virtual address services are not accepted for that.

Who Mercury accepts

Mercury's eligibility page says you do not need to be a US citizen or resident to apply. The company itself must meet three conditions:

  • It is formed and registered in the United States or a US territory, for example a Wyoming or Delaware LLC.
  • It has existing or planned operations in the US.
  • It has a US or international address for its principal place of business.

Mercury also keeps a list of countries and regions where founders cannot currently be supported, and it does not serve some industries, such as money services businesses, cannabis, adult entertainment and internet gambling. Check Mercury's current eligibility and prohibited countries pages before you apply, because these lists change.

Address 1: the company's principal place of business

This is where the business is actually run from. Mercury's wording is that residential addresses are accepted, but registered agent addresses, P.O. boxes and UPS Store addresses are not. A few practical points follow from that:

  • It does not have to be in the US. If you run the company from your home office abroad, that address is acceptable as the principal place of business.
  • Your registered agent's address is not a business address. The registered agent receives legal papers for the state. It is not where you operate. See registered agent vs business address.
  • Mail drops are a common problem. Many virtual mailbox addresses are flagged as commercial mail receiving agencies (CMRAs) in address databases. See what a CMRA address is and why virtual mailboxes cause bank rejections.

Mercury's separate guidance for accountant-managed accounts also describes a legal address, the one on your formation documents, which must be in the US and cannot be a P.O. box, PMB or virtual office. If your formation documents list only a registered agent, expect Mercury to ask for a separate physical address for the business.

Address 2: each owner's home address

Every beneficial owner, meaning each person who owns 25% or more directly or through other entities, must give the physical address where they currently live. Mercury says PO boxes, mail drops, and commercial mail receiving or virtual address services cannot be used here, and it may ask for a document that verifies the address. Documents Mercury lists for this include:

  • A valid government-issued ID or international passport.
  • A bank, credit or other account statement from the last 60 days (not from Mercury).
  • A utility bill or subscription invoice from the last 60 days.
  • Your most recent tax documents or pay stub.

For a founder living outside the US, the answer is simple: enter your real home address in your own country. Never give a bank an address you do not live at as your personal residential address.

Documents Mercury asks for

  • The company's official formation documents.
  • The IRS-issued EIN document. If your EIN is still pending, Mercury says you can apply with your completed Form SS-4 and send the official EIN document within 90 days of approval. See how to get an EIN without an SSN.
  • A government ID, such as a passport, for at least one person with operating control and for each owner of 25% or more.
  • Details about your current or planned US operations and your source of funds.

How to keep your application consistent

  1. Use the same legal name and EIN everywhere, exactly as they appear on your IRS letter.
  2. Give a real principal place of business that is not your registered agent, a PO box or a UPS Store.
  3. Give your true home address as the owner's address, even when it is outside the US.
  4. Describe your US operations or plans clearly: customers, suppliers, payment processors or contractors in the US.
  5. Keep the same business address on your state filing, IRS records, bank and payment processor where possible.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a non-US resident open a Mercury account for a US LLC?

Usually yes. Mercury says you do not need to be a US citizen or resident. The company must be formed in the US or a US territory, have existing or planned US operations, and have an acceptable principal business address. Founders in some countries cannot currently be supported.

Can I use my registered agent address with Mercury?

Not as your principal place of business. Mercury says registered agent addresses, PO boxes and UPS Store addresses are not accepted for that address.

Does Mercury accept a foreign address for the business?

Yes. Mercury accepts a US or international address for the principal place of business, and residential addresses are accepted.

What address does Mercury want for the owner?

The physical address where each 25% or more owner currently lives. Mercury does not accept PO boxes, mail drops, or commercial mail receiving or virtual address services for owner addresses, and may ask for a document to verify it.

Can I apply to Mercury before my EIN arrives?

Mercury says you can apply with your completed Form SS-4 if the EIN is pending, then send the official EIN document within 90 days of approval.
