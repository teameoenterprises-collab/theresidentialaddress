Stripe Account for a Non-US LLC Owner: Requirements and Common Issues

💳🏢🌐

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: Yes, a non-US person who owns a registered US LLC can usually open a US Stripe account. Stripe requires the business to be registered in the US and verifies the legal entity name, entity type, EIN, the representative's tax ID and the business address. A PO box is not accepted. If the representative has no SSN or ITIN, Stripe asks for their local non-US tax ID and documents showing they are not a US taxpayer.

What Stripe requires for a US account

Stripe's own help center lists the rules for US accounts. The ones that matter most to foreign founders are:

  • A US-registered business. Registered businesses must be registered in the US, even if you operate from another country. An LLC formed in Wyoming, Delaware or another state meets this.
  • An EIN. Stripe verifies the company's EIN against its legal name, so the name must match your IRS records exactly.
  • A physical business address. Stripe asks for the address of the physical location where most of your business activity happens, and PO boxes are not accepted.
  • A verified representative. Every US account has individuals attached to it who are verified, including the person who opens the account.

Stripe can ask for official documents to confirm any of these details. Requirements change, so check Stripe's current support pages before you apply.

Do you need an SSN or ITIN?

Stripe expects US individuals to enter their SSN, or an ITIN if they cannot get an SSN. For a representative who is not a US taxpayer and has neither number, Stripe's help center says it expects the person's local non-US tax ID plus documentation confirming they are not a US taxpayer.

Two points to know before you rely on that route:

  • Stripe says that once an account reaches $500,000 in lifetime volume, all listed owners must provide the last 4 digits of their SSN or ITIN, and in some cases the full number.
  • Accounts set up as Individual or Sole Proprietor with a non-US representative have been asked to provide a US TIN. A registered LLC is the more stable structure for a foreign owner.

Because of these rules, many foreign founders apply for an ITIN early rather than waiting for Stripe to request one. See how to get an ITIN as a non-resident.

The address question

Stripe's wording is "the physical location where the majority of your business activity is carried out." Keep these points in mind:

  • A registered agent's address exists to receive legal documents for the state. It is not where your business operates. See registered agent vs business address.
  • Many virtual mailbox addresses are flagged as commercial mail receiving agencies (CMRAs) in address databases, which is one reason they are sometimes questioned. See what a CMRA address is.
  • Use the same business address on your state filing, your IRS records, your bank account and Stripe. Mismatches are a common cause of extra verification requests.
  • Your personal details must be true. Enter your real residential address where Stripe asks for the representative's own address, even if it is outside the US.

Setup order that avoids most problems

  1. Form the LLC and wait for state approval.
  2. Get the EIN with Form SS-4. See how to get an EIN without an SSN.
  3. Open a US business bank account in the LLC's name for payouts. See opening a US business bank account as a non-resident.
  4. Apply to Stripe with the legal name, EIN and address exactly as they appear on your formation and IRS documents.
  5. Answer document requests promptly and describe your products and website accurately.

Common reasons for delays

  • The legal name in Stripe does not match the EIN letter (CP 575 or 147C).
  • A PO box entered as the business address.
  • Choosing Individual or Sole Proprietor instead of LLC as the business type.
  • A website without clear pricing, refund terms or contact details.
  • Prohibited or restricted products under Stripe's terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreigner open a Stripe account with a US LLC?

Usually yes. Stripe requires the business to be registered in the US, with an EIN and a physical business address, and verifies the people on the account. The owner does not need to live in the US.

Does Stripe require an SSN for a non-US LLC owner?

Not always. Stripe accepts an SSN or ITIN, and for a representative who is not a US taxpayer it asks for a local non-US tax ID and supporting documents. Stripe can ask owners for an SSN or ITIN once an account passes $500,000 in lifetime volume.

Can I use a PO box as my Stripe business address?

No. Stripe's help center says PO boxes are not accepted. It asks for the physical location where most of your business activity happens.

Do I need a US bank account for Stripe?

For a US Stripe account, payouts go to a US bank account, so most foreign-owned LLCs open a US business account in the company's name before applying.
