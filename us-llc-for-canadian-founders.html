How Canadian Founders Can Form and Run a US LLC (2026 Guide)

🇨🇦🇺🇸💼

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: Yes. A Canadian resident can form and own a US LLC without US citizenship or residency, and the US and Canada have an income tax treaty in force. The catch is classification: the CRA's longstanding position is that an LLC is a corporation for Canadian tax, while the IRS treats a single-member LLC as disregarded by default. That mismatch can create double tax, so get advice from a Canada-US cross-border adviser before you start trading through the LLC.

Can a Canadian own a US LLC?

Yes. No US state requires LLC owners to be US citizens or residents. You can file with the state online from Toronto, Vancouver or anywhere else, appoint a registered agent in that state and apply for an EIN. Canadian founders often look at a US LLC to sell to US customers, contract with US clients or hold a US bank account in a US entity's name.

Is there a Canada-US tax treaty?

Yes. Canada is on the IRS list of countries with which the US has an income tax treaty. Treaty benefits depend on who is treated as earning the income, and that is exactly where US LLCs get complicated for Canadians, so do not plan around a treaty benefit until an adviser confirms it applies to your structure.

How the CRA treats a US LLC

RSM Canada summarises the CRA's longstanding position as treating an LLC as a corporation for Canadian tax purposes, even though the LLC can be fiscally transparent for US tax. In practice that means:

  • The US may tax the LLC's income as yours, because a single-member LLC is disregarded for US income tax by default.
  • Canada may see a foreign corporation and tax you when income reaches you, or under its foreign affiliate rules, rather than on the LLC's income as it is earned.
  • Because the two countries are looking at different taxpayers and different timing, foreign tax credits may not line up, which is how double tax arises.

Advisers sometimes compare an LLC with other structures, or with a US tax election, for Canadian owners. Which one fits depends on your income, so this is a decision to make with professional help, not from a checklist.

Canadian reporting to know about

Because the CRA generally sees an LLC as a non-resident corporation, it can be a foreign affiliate of its Canadian owner. The CRA's Form T1134 is the information return for foreign affiliates. For tax years starting after 2020 it is due within 10 months of the end of the reporting taxpayer's tax year, and there is an exception for dormant affiliates where the total cost of your interests is under CAD 100,000. Your accountant can confirm whether T1134 or other foreign reporting applies to you.

US filings for a Canadian-owned LLC

  1. EIN. Without an SSN or ITIN you cannot use the IRS online form, so you file Form SS-4 by fax or mail, or apply by phone as an international applicant. Steps are in EIN without an SSN.
  2. Form 5472 with a pro forma 1120. Required for most foreign-owned single-member LLCs in any year with reportable transactions, such as you putting money in or taking money out. The penalty for missing it is $25,000. See Form 5472 explained.
  3. No BOI report. FinCEN's final rule, effective August 14, 2026, exempts companies formed in a US state. See BOI in 2026.
  4. ITIN only when needed. You do not need one to form the LLC. It can become necessary if you must file a US personal return. See ITIN for non-residents.

Banking and Stripe

US bank and fintech policies for owners in Canada vary. Some providers accept LLCs with foreign owners online, some require a branch visit, and some do not serve non-resident owners at all. Read each provider's eligibility page and see our guide to US business bank accounts for non-residents.

  • Expect to provide the LLC's formation documents, EIN confirmation, operating agreement and your passport.
  • Your personal residential address on any application should be your real home address in Canada.
  • Many banks decline business addresses that are mail drops or virtual mailboxes. See CMRA addresses.

Stripe operates in Canada, so a Canadian business can use Stripe without a US entity. If you want US payouts in the LLC's name, a US Stripe account opened by the LLC is the usual route. See Stripe for non-US LLC owners.

Which state Canadian founders choose

Most non-resident founders choose Wyoming or Delaware. Wyoming has low yearly fees and no state income tax, and Delaware is common for companies raising US investment. See Wyoming LLC for non-residents. If you will have staff, an office or inventory in a particular state, that state may also require registration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Canadian citizen living in Canada form a US LLC?

Yes. US states do not require LLC owners to live in the US or hold US citizenship. You need a registered agent in the state and an EIN from the IRS.

How does the CRA treat a US LLC?

The CRA's longstanding position is that an LLC is a corporation for Canadian tax purposes, even when the US treats it as transparent. This mismatch can cause double tax, so get cross-border advice.

Does the Canada-US tax treaty apply to my LLC?

Canada and the US have an income tax treaty in force, but whether its benefits apply to LLC income depends on how the income and the LLC are classified. Confirm with an adviser before relying on it.

Do I need to file Form T1134 for my US LLC?

Possibly. The CRA uses Form T1134 for foreign affiliates, and it generally sees an LLC as a foreign corporation. There is an exception for some dormant affiliates, so ask your accountant.
