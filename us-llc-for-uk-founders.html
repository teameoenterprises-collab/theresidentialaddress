How UK Founders Can Form and Run a US LLC (2026 Guide)

🇬🇧🇺🇸📝

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: Yes. A UK resident can form and own a US LLC from Britain, with no US citizenship, visa or residency. The US and UK have an income tax treaty in force, but HMRC generally treats a US LLC as an opaque company, while the IRS treats a single-member LLC as a disregarded entity for income tax by default. That difference is the main reason UK founders should take cross-border tax advice before trading through an LLC.

Can a UK resident own a US LLC?

Yes. US states do not require LLC members to be US citizens or residents. A founder in London, Manchester or Edinburgh files formation papers online with the chosen state, appoints a registered agent in that state and then applies for an Employer Identification Number (EIN). Nothing in the process requires you to travel to the US.

What changes for a UK founder is not the US paperwork, which is the same for any non-resident, but how the LLC fits into your UK tax position.

Is there a US-UK tax treaty?

Yes. The United Kingdom appears on the IRS list of countries with which the US has an income tax treaty. A treaty can affect how certain income is taxed and which country has the first right to tax it, but whether it helps in your case depends on the type of income and on how each country classifies the LLC. Do not assume the treaty removes UK tax on LLC profits.

How HMRC views a US LLC

HMRC's International Manual keeps a list of foreign entities with its view of whether each one is transparent or opaque for UK tax. The US limited liability company is listed as opaque, meaning HMRC generally treats it like a company rather than looking through it to the members.

  • The IRS, by contrast, treats a single-member LLC as disregarded for US income tax unless it elects to be taxed as a corporation.
  • In 2015 the UK Supreme Court decided Anson v HMRC, a case about double tax relief for US tax paid on Delaware LLC profits. In Revenue and Customs Brief 15 (2015), HMRC said the decision was specific to the facts of that case and that it would continue its existing approach of treating US LLCs as companies.
  • The practical risk is a mismatch: the US and the UK may look at different taxpayers, or tax the income at different times, which can make double tax relief harder to claim.

How profits and withdrawals are taxed in the UK for your specific LLC is a question for a UK adviser who works with US entities.

US filings a UK owner should expect

  • EIN: If you have no SSN or ITIN, the IRS online application is not available, so you apply with Form SS-4 by fax or mail, or by phone as an international applicant. See how to get an EIN without an SSN.
  • Form 5472: A single-member LLC owned by a non-US person generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 each year it has reportable transactions with its owner, such as funding the bank account. The penalty for not filing is $25,000. See Form 5472 for foreign-owned LLCs.
  • BOI: Under FinCEN's final rule effective August 14, 2026, companies formed in a US state no longer file beneficial ownership reports. See BOI reporting in 2026.
  • ITIN: Not needed to form the LLC or get an EIN. You may need one later, for example to file a US personal return. See getting an ITIN as a non-resident.

Banking and payments from the UK

Some US banks and fintech providers open business accounts for LLCs with owners abroad, and others require an owner to visit a branch or to live in the US. Policies change often, so check each provider's eligibility page before applying. Our guide to opening a US business bank account as a non-resident covers the usual documents: Articles of Organization, EIN letter, operating agreement and your passport.

Many banks decline accounts where the only business address is a commercial mail receiving agency (CMRA), such as a mailbox store or virtual mailbox. See what a CMRA address is. When a bank asks for your personal residential address, give your real UK home address.

Stripe supports businesses in the UK directly, so some founders keep a UK account for UK customers. A US LLC can open a US Stripe account in the LLC's name. See Stripe for non-US LLC owners.

Which state UK founders usually pick

Wyoming and Delaware are the most common choices for non-resident founders. Wyoming is often chosen for low annual cost and no state income tax, while Delaware is the usual choice for companies planning to raise US venture capital. See Wyoming vs Delaware.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I form a US LLC while living in the UK?

Yes. US states have no citizenship or residency requirement for LLC members. You need a registered agent in the state of formation and an EIN from the IRS.

Does HMRC treat a US LLC as transparent?

Generally no. HMRC's International Manual lists the US LLC as opaque, so HMRC usually treats it like a company. Your own facts can matter, so take advice from a UK adviser who works with US entities.

Is there a tax treaty between the US and the UK?

Yes. The United Kingdom is on the IRS list of countries with a US income tax treaty in force. Whether it reduces tax on your LLC income depends on the income and on how each country classifies the LLC.

Do I still file Form 5472 if I live in the UK?

Usually yes. A foreign-owned single-member LLC generally files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 every year it has reportable transactions with its owner, wherever the owner lives.
