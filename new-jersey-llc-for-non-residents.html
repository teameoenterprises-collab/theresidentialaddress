How to Form a New Jersey LLC as a Non-Resident (2026 Cost and Steps)

🌉📄💵

Last updated November 2026

Short answer: Anyone can form a New Jersey LLC from abroad, with no US citizenship, residency or visa. You file a Certificate of Formation with the New Jersey Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services for $100, name a registered agent with a New Jersey address, and complete the state tax registration (Form NJ-REG). Each year you file a $75 annual report by the last day of the month in which the LLC was formed.

What New Jersey charges

  • Certificate of Formation: $100, according to the Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services fee schedule.
  • Annual report: $75, plus a small payment service fee when paying online by card or e-check.
  • Reinstatement of a revoked LLC: $75 plus all unpaid annual reports.
  • EIN: free from the IRS.

Step by step

  1. Check the name with the state's business name search.
  2. Get an EIN. The IRS asks you to form the LLC with the state first and then apply for the EIN, because Form SS-4 needs the LLC's legal name and formation date. New Jersey's registration guide mentions the EIN early, so many founders file the Certificate of Formation and the Form SS-4 back to back. Without an SSN or ITIN you apply with Form SS-4 by fax or mail, so plan for the wait.
  3. Name a registered agent with a New Jersey address. It can be you or someone else, but the address must be in New Jersey. Registered agent services have a state-issued number that you enter on the form.
  4. File the Certificate of Formation online.
  5. Register for state taxes with Form NJ-REG. Once both filings are done you can get a Business Registration Certificate.
  6. Sign an operating agreement and open a bank account.

The yearly annual report

New Jersey's business portal says the annual report is due every year on the last day of the month in which you formed the business. The state sends reminders before the due date. If an LLC misses two years of annual reports, it can be revoked and must file the missing reports and pay a reinstatement fee to return to good standing.

Privacy: what is public

New Jersey business records are public. The state's status reports show the registered agent, business addresses, the date of the last annual report and any managers listed on filings. Assume that details you put on state filings can be seen by others.

Federal steps for non-resident owners

These apply to every US LLC, whatever the state.

  • EIN: without an SSN or ITIN you apply with Form SS-4 by fax or mail. See how to get an EIN without an SSN.
  • Form 5472: a single-member LLC owned by a non-US person files Form 5472 with a pro forma Form 1120 each year, due April 15, even with no income. See Form 5472 for foreign-owned LLCs.
  • BOI: LLCs formed in the US, including New Jersey LLCs, are exempt from FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting under the final rule effective August 14, 2026. See BOI reporting in 2026.

Registered agent address vs business address

The registered agent address is where the state and courts send legal notices. It must be a physical street address in New Jersey. It is not automatically your business or mailing address, and many agents do not forward everyday mail or let you use their address for banking.

Banks and payment providers usually ask for a business address and for each owner's real personal residential address, which for most non-residents is their home abroad. Many banks decline CMRA or virtual mailbox addresses as a business address, so check the bank's eligibility page before you apply. See what a CMRA address is.

New Jersey vs Wyoming or Delaware

New Jersey's state fees are moderate, $100 to form and $75 a year, but it has an extra tax registration step and state income taxes. It makes sense if you, your team or your operations are in New Jersey, since an LLC doing business there would need to register there anyway. For a remote online business with no US base, Wyoming is the usual choice, and Delaware suits companies planning to raise venture capital. See Wyoming vs Delaware.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a non-US citizen form a New Jersey LLC?

Yes. New Jersey has no citizenship or residency requirement for LLC owners. You need a registered agent with a New Jersey address.

How much does a New Jersey LLC cost?

The Certificate of Formation costs $100 and the annual report costs $75 a year, before registered agent fees.

When is the New Jersey annual report due?

By the last day of the month in which the LLC was formed, every year.

Do I need an EIN before forming a New Jersey LLC?

No. Form the LLC with the state first, then apply for the EIN with Form SS-4, as the IRS recommends. Non-residents without an SSN or ITIN file Form SS-4 by fax or mail.
